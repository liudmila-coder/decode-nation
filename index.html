Why We Exist

Women are half the population, yet the world we live in still doesn't fully reflect their lives.

Women have monthly cycles, physical needs, and experiences that shape how they work, travel, study, and move through everyday life. These realities should be a natural part of how society works - not something women are expected to navigate on their own.

Our company exists to close that gap and make women more visible in the world around us.

Our First Project – Essential Station

Wall-Mounted Retail Station for Women’s Essentials

Essential Stations address a very familiar situation: getting your period unexpectedly, having a leak or cramps, or simply realizing you need something while you’re away from home.

Placed near or inside restroom areas in high-traffic locations, the station gives women a quick way to purchase the essentials they need right there.

Proposed inventory

Pads · Tampons · Liners · Intimate wipes · Heating patches · Underwear / Emergency kit

We handle the station from end to end: installation, product selection, inventory, payments, restocking, maintenance, and day-to-day operation. The location provides wall space and access to a standard outlet.

About Us

Two founders. One mission.Two ways of seeing the same problem

We became friends first and business partners later. Decode Nation grew from conversations about women’s real experiences and from realizing that we approach the same questions from very different directions.

Liudmila Rukavishnikova

Co-Founder, Creative & Vision Lead

Liudmila is driven by the question of what makes a woman feel fully alive. She notices the parts of women’s lives that are often normalized, hidden, or difficult to put into words - and wants to make them visible.

Her background includes Human Resources, PCM training, and creating immersive tours in Los Angeles. These different fields have shaped her understanding of people, communication, and how meaningful experiences are created.

Sofiia Blazhko

Co-Founder, Research & Strategy Lead

Sofiia likes to understand how things work. She asks questions, looks at different perspectives, and turns information into clear structure. Her own struggles with severe PMS led her to ask deeper questions about women’s health and start listening to what other women have to say.

In the future, she wants to take those questions further through research - to understand what women face and use that knowledge to build better solutions.

September 24, 2026

Our first official networking event was the AMAC Los Angeles Fall Soirée. AMAC (Airport Minority Advisory Council) supports opportunities for minority- and women-owned businesses in the aviation industry.

We had the chance to meet leaders from LAX, Ontario, and Long Beach airports, learn about upcoming developments, and gain valuable insights into bringing our Essential Stations to airport spaces.

What made this experience special was seeing our ideas move beyond the planning stage and become part of real conversations. Just a few months after launching Decode Nation, it felt like an important first step, full of new connections and possibilities.

We're grateful for the warm welcome and look forward to staying involved with the AMAC community, building relationships, and sharing what comes next.
